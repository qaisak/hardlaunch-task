# Long overlay: the house style

A long overlay is a TikTok where a real person reacts silently (deadpan, small head shake, eating,
sitting in a car) while a single block of text sits on screen for the whole video. The text IS the
content. The viewer reads it, the person's face is the punctuation.

Reference piece (a study tool adapted to a calorie tracking app):

"just seen a girl trying to track her calories. no scales. no scoops. no logbook. no cal ai. just her
and her judgement against the world. Like a psychopath"

What makes it work, and what every overlay must have:

1. **Observational opening.** Someone was seen doing the painful thing the old way. Third person,
   past tense, lowercase: "just seen a guy...", "my flatmate...", "watched a girl at the gym...".
   First-person confession is the only allowed alternative: "i did my whole dissertation without...".
2. **The "no X. no Y. no Z." list.** Three to five short fragments naming the tools, shortcuts or
   crutches the person is NOT using. Each is a real thing people in this niche use. The brand goes
   in this list, lowercase, as one item, or lands in the twist. Never explain the brand.
3. **The absurd escalation.** "just her and her judgement against the world." One line that makes
   the manual way sound heroic and insane at the same time.
4. **The deadpan verdict.** A short closing judgement that invites people to argue or tag someone:
   "like a psychopath", "respect honestly", "couldn't be me", "she's built different and wrong".
5. **Length 30 to 70 words.** One block. Lowercase throughout except the first letter of the verdict
   if it reads better. Full stops, no commas where a full stop works. No emojis. No hashtags.
   No exclamation marks. No em dashes.
6. **Niche-specific nouns.** The list and the scene must use the exact objects, apps and habits of
   THIS niche. "no scales. no scoops." works for calories. For a study app it would be
   "no notes. no flashcards. no chatgpt." If the nouns would fit another niche, it is not on-brand.
7. **The brand is never sold.** No benefit claims, no adjectives, no "the best". The brand appears
   as a thing normal people already use, which is the whole persuasion.

Common failures to avoid:
- Reads like an ad or a testimonial ("this app changed my life")
- Generic nouns that fit any niche
- More than one idea. It is one person, one moment, one verdict.
- Over 70 words. Nobody reads a wall.
- Explaining the joke.

Two more rules:
- **Match the brand's market.** If the site prices in $ or the audience is American, the scene, slang
  and places are American (the L train, a Trader Joe's, "dude"). If it is £ or UK, British. If global or
  unclear, keep places generic (the bus, the library, the gym) and avoid slang that dates it to one country.
- **Invent the verdict.** The verdict examples above ("like a psychopath", "built different and wrong")
  are illustrations of the register, not a menu. Write a new one for this scene every time.
