# sample_transcript: ranked variants

## 1. [SHIP] v1 - Number-first cost confession, single take to camera  (score 8/10)

**Hook** visual: Founder holding one shoe, sole facing the lens, warehouse behind | audio: It costs us ninety pounds more per pair to make this shoe, and we eat most of it. | text: **£90 more per pair**

**Script**
- It costs us ninety pounds more per pair to make this shoe, and we eat most of it.
- Everyone asks why we don't just make them in Vietnam like everyone else.
- The factory we use is in Portugal. Family owned, third generation, forty people.
- They've made shoes for brands you'd recognise. They're not allowed to tell me which.
- Here's the bit I can prove.
- Industry return rate on running shoes is around eleven percent.
- Ours is under three.
- That gap is the ninety quid. We just moved it to the front of the process instead of the refund queue.

**On-screen text:** Third generation. 40 people. / Industry returns: ~11% / Ours: under 3% / Same money. Different end of the process.

**Caption:** Would you rather we spent it on the shoe or the returns team?  
**~27s**

**Share reason:** People who follow manufacturing and supply chain content send this to friends who bang on about where things are made  
**Comment trigger:** The unnamed big brands made in the same factory. People will guess in the comments

**Why views:** A precise cost number spoken as a confession in the first second creates an immediate why, and the return rate pays it off before anyone can scroll.

Scores: hook_strength 5, specificity 5, retention 4, share_trigger 4, native 4, honesty 5, reusability 4

Editor fix: Cut "Here's the bit I can prove" and land the 11% vs 3% by second 8, the factory backstory is the flattest part and currently sits in the middle where scroll happens.

## 2. [bench] v2 - Object as proof, result before the story  (score 7/10)

**Hook** visual: Beaten shoe dropped onto a table, camera pushes into the sole tread | audio: This one's done twelve hundred kilometres. | text: **1,200 km. Look at the sole.**

**Script**
- This one's done twelve hundred kilometres.
- That's the sole. Flip it over, that's the upper.
- I'm not going to tell you it looks new, it doesn't. But it's still fine.
- The reason isn't the rubber, it's the last.
- The last is the foot shaped mould the shoe gets built on.
- Most big brands run a handful of lasts across their entire range.
- We scanned about four hundred runners' feet and built our own.
- Wider toe box than you're used to. Nothing in the shoe is fighting your foot.
- That's why this one's still here after twelve hundred kilometres.

**On-screen text:** The 'last' = the mould the shoe is built on / Big brands: a handful of lasts for everything / Us: 400 runners' feet, scanned / Wider toe box

**Caption:** How far has your current pair actually gone?  
**~30s**

**Share reason:** Runners send this to the friend who replaces their shoes every six months on principle  
**Comment trigger:** Everyone posts their own mileage and argues about when shoes are 'dead'

**Why views:** A worn object on screen is instant evidence, and opening with the result means the explanation feels earned rather than pitched.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 4, retention 4, share_trigger 4, native 5, honesty 5, reusability 4

Editor fix: The two-line definition of "the last" is a lecture beat, compress it to one on-screen label and get to the 400 scanned feet faster so the middle doesn't sag.

## 3. [bench] v4 - Two contradictory quotes on screen, silent build, reveal last  (score 7/10)

**Hook** visual: Black screen, white text appears, hands lacing a shoe underneath | audio: silent / trending audio | text: **"This feels weird."**

**Script**
- First time anyone puts these on, they say the same thing.
- It feels weird.
- Second run, same person, different sentence.
- It feels like nothing.
- That second one is the whole goal.
- Weird is just your foot noticing there's room in the toe box for once.
- We built the mould from scans of about four hundred runners' feet instead of borrowing an old one.
- You're not meant to feel a running shoe.
- Which is why the first time, it feels weird.

**On-screen text:** "This feels weird." (run 1) / "This feels like nothing." (run 2) / Same person. Same shoe. / 400 runners' feet, scanned / You're not meant to feel a shoe

**Caption:** Weird for one run, then nothing. Worth it?  
**~24s**

**Share reason:** Wide toe box people send this to friends who complain about cramped shoes and blackened toenails  
**Comment trigger:** Direct disagreement about whether a shoe should feel like anything at all, plus people describing their own break-in experience

**Why views:** A quoted complaint as the opening line looks like criticism, so viewers stay to see how it gets resolved, and the last line loops back to the first.

Scores: hook_strength 3, specificity 4, retention 4, share_trigger 4, native 4, honesty 5, reusability 5

Editor fix: Black screen kills the first 1.5s, open on the foot going into the shoe with "This feels weird" already on screen so there's something to look at while the quote lands.

## 4. [bench] v3 - Stated hot take, audience split live  (score 6/10)

**Hook** visual: Founder mid shrug, close crop, no product visible yet | audio: Forty percent of you are going to hate this, and that's fine. | text: **40% of you will hate this**

**Script**
- Forty percent of you are going to hate this, and that's fine.
- We don't do sales. Ever. No Black Friday, no twenty percent off code in a creator's bio.
- The price is the price.
- Here's why I can't move it.
- The factory's in Portugal, family run, third generation, forty people.
- It costs about ninety pounds more per pair than making them elsewhere, and we absorb most of that.
- There's no margin sitting there waiting to be discounted off.
- A sale would just mean I'd been overcharging you in November.
- So, forty percent of you, off you go. Genuinely fine.

**On-screen text:** No sales. No codes. No Black Friday. / +£90 per pair to make / A discount = you were overcharged before / Still here?

**Caption:** Never discounting: principled or just annoying?  
**~26s**

**Share reason:** Sent between people who argue about pricing ethics and the discount treadmill, plus founders sending it to other founders  
**Comment trigger:** A plainly stated hot take that splits the room, and the video literally invites the 40% to say so

**Why views:** Naming a percentage of the audience who will disagree in the first second makes both camps stay to find out which one they're in.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 4, retention 3, share_trigger 4, native 3, honesty 3, reusability 4

Editor fix: Strip the invented specifics (Black Friday, 20% creator codes) and stop the brand-manifesto tone, then show the shoe in the first second so it isn't 26 seconds of a man defending his pricing to camera.
