## 1. [SHIP] v2 - POV text overlay, silent, trending audio, shot in real conditions  (score 8/10)

**Hook** visual: Dark bedroom, face lit only by phone screen, eyes open, ceiling above | audio: silent / trending audio | text: **3:07am. again.**

**Script**
- silent throughout — text overlays carry the whole story over slow handheld shots in the dark

**On-screen text:** 3:07am. again. / not stressed. not thirsty. just awake. / falling asleep was never my problem / staying asleep was / so I stopped buying sleep sprays and gummies / and tried 300mg magnesium glycinate. one capsule. / nights 1-6: nothing at all / night 10: didn't see 3am / still don't

**Caption:** 3am club, what's your exact time?  
**~22s**

**Share reason:** Sent silently at 2am from one insomniac to another as an 'this is literally me' tag — no audio needed, works on mute in bed  
**Comment trigger:** 'What's your exact time' makes hundreds of people post 3:04, 3:12, 4am — cheap, high-volume comments and an argument about why it's always 3am

**Why views:** Filmed in the exact situation the viewer is in while scrolling — dark room, phone light, 3:07am on screen — so the hook is recognition rather than information, which holds mute viewers.

Scores: hook_strength 5, specificity 4, retention 3, share_trigger 5, native 5, honesty 4, reusability 5

Editor fix: Text-only over 'slow handheld shots in the dark' will flatline in the middle — specify a distinct visual beat per card (phone clock, ceiling, bin of sprays/gummies, capsule in palm, curtains at morning) so something changes every 2s, otherwise this dies at second 8 despite the best hook here.

## 2. [SHIP] v3 - Ranking with the controversial pick last: '4 things I tried, ranked worst to best'  (score 8/10)

**Hook** visual: Four items lined up on a duvet: a lavender spray, a weighted eye mask, a sleep tea box, a plain white capsule bottle. Hand sweeps the spray straight off the bed. | audio: Four things I bought to fix waking up at 3am, ranked, and number one is going to annoy people. | text: **Ranked. #1 will annoy people.**

**Script**
- Four things I bought to fix waking up at 3am, ranked, and number one is going to annoy people.
- Four: the pillow spray. Smells lovely. Did absolutely nothing at 3am.
- Three: the sleep tea. Made me get up at 3am to wee instead. Worse.
- Two: the weighted eye mask. Genuinely nice. Helped me fall asleep, not stay asleep.
- One: the boring one. 300mg magnesium glycinate, one capsule, 30 minutes before bed.
- No melatonin, so no hangover feeling in the morning, which was my whole issue with the other stuff.
- Took me about a week and a half before I noticed anything, so don't judge it on night two.
- The reviews are all the same sentence: 'I stopped waking up at 3am.' That was it for me too.
- Annoying, because I wanted the answer to be the eye mask. It was the £24 bottle of boring.
- So: four things, and the one I'd buy again is the one with no smell, no ritual, no vibe.

**On-screen text:** 4. pillow spray — nice smell, nothing / 3. sleep tea — made it worse / 2. eye mask — falling asleep only / 1. the boring capsule / gave it 10 days before judging

**Caption:** Defend the pillow spray in the comments, I dare you.  
**~38s**

**Share reason:** Sent to the friend who owns all four of these things and swears by the spray, as a wind-up  
**Comment trigger:** Openly ranking beloved products last — pillow spray and sleep tea fans will argue, and people will add their own #1

**Why views:** A four-item ranking creates four micro-payoffs plus a pre-announced controversial ending, so the viewer stays for the reveal instead of dropping at the product mention.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 4, retention 5, share_trigger 5, native 4, honesty 4, reusability 5

Editor fix: Lines 8-10 are three closing lines doing one job and the review-quote line is where it starts smelling like an ad — cut 'the reviews are all the same sentence' and end on 'I wanted it to be the eye mask, it was the £24 bottle of boring' so the last line loops back to 'number one is going to annoy people'.

## 3. [bench] v4 - Anti-sell: 'reasons you should not buy this' — objections stated before the pitch  (score 8/10)

**Hook** visual: Creator holding the bottle, then setting it down and pushing it away from the camera | audio: Three reasons you shouldn't buy this, from someone who takes it every night. | text: **Don't buy this if...**

**Script**
- Three reasons you shouldn't buy this, from someone who takes it every night.
- One: it's slow. It took me about nine nights. If you want tonight to be different, this isn't it.
- Two: it's £24 a month on subscription. That's a real cost for something this undramatic.
- Three: it doesn't knock you out. There's no melatonin in it. You won't feel drugged.
- If falling asleep is your problem, I'm honestly not sure this is your thing.
- Mine was staying asleep. Awake at 3am, every night, for about two years.
- One capsule, 300mg magnesium glycinate, half an hour before bed, nothing else changed.
- Two thousand-odd reviews and they all say the same boring sentence about 3am. So does mine now.
- So don't buy it for tonight. Buy it if you're willing to be unimpressed for a week.

**On-screen text:** 1. it's slow — ~9 nights for me / 2. £24/month, real money / 3. won't knock you out / no melatonin = no morning fog / be unimpressed for a week

**Caption:** Would you give something 10 nights before deciding?  
**~30s**

**Share reason:** Sceptics send it to other sceptics — it's the rare supplement video that leads with the downsides, so it reads as safe to forward  
**Comment trigger:** Stating the price out loud splits the comments into 'that's expensive' vs 'cheaper than my sleep', and 'this isn't for you if...' makes people declare which problem they have

**Why views:** Opening by pushing the product away inverts the expected ad structure, so the sceptical viewer stays to hear the criticism they were already forming.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 5, retention 4, share_trigger 4, native 5, honesty 4, reusability 5

Editor fix: 'Two thousand-odd reviews all say the same boring sentence' is the one line that flips it back into a pitch — either quote the sentence flat with no framing or drop it; the anti-sell only works if you never sell, and the £24 objection is doing more persuasion than the proof line is.

## 4. [bench] v1 - Result-first self-test: 'I tried X for N nights, the outcome stated in line one'  (score 7/10)

**Hook** visual: Phone screen filmed at arm's length showing sleep-tracker graph, finger pointing at a flat stretch where the old 3am gap used to be | audio: Night four was still rubbish. Night nine is the reason I'm filming this. | text: **Night 4: rubbish. Night 9: this.**

**Script**
- Night four was still rubbish. Night nine is the reason I'm filming this.
- I've woken up at 3am, basically every night, for about two years.
- Not stressed. Not needing the loo. Just awake, staring at the ceiling.
- So I tried one capsule of magnesium glycinate, 30 minutes before bed. That's it.
- Nothing happened for the first five nights and I nearly gave up.
- Then one morning I realised I hadn't seen 3am on my phone in a while.
- It's 300mg, there's no melatonin in it, and I don't wake up foggy.
- It's not magic. It took a week and a half to notice anything.
- But I haven't checked the time in the middle of the night since night nine.

**On-screen text:** two years of 3am / one capsule, 30 min before bed / nights 1-5: nothing / no melatonin in it / still boring. still works.

**Caption:** What time do you wake up at? Mine was always 3am.  
**~32s**

**Share reason:** Someone who wakes up at 3am sends this to their partner who watches them do it, or to the group chat where everyone complains about being tired  
**Comment trigger:** The stated 3am time makes people name their own wake-up time; the 'nothing happened for five nights' admission invites 'how long did it actually take you'

**Why views:** Opening with a dated before/after on a real tracker screen shows the result before any context, and the admission that nights 1-5 failed buys credibility from a sceptical audience in the first second.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 4, retention 4, share_trigger 4, native 5, honesty 4, reusability 4

Editor fix: The hook says 'night nine is the reason I'm filming this' but never shows night nine — put the tracker graph reveal (flat stretch where the 3am gap was) as a hard cut at the end to close the loop visually, and cut line 3 ('not stressed, not needing the loo') into an on-screen text line so the first 5 seconds move faster.

## 5. [bench] v6 - Mid-process diary entry: 'day N of something, filmed at the moment of doubt'  (score 7/10)

**Hook** visual: Creator in the kitchen at night, unwashed hair, holding a capsule between finger and thumb up to the camera light | audio: Day six of this and I'm about to say something I'll probably regret. | text: **Day 6. Still nothing.**

**Script**
- Day six of this and I'm about to say something I'll probably regret.
- I think it's not working. Six nights, one capsule each night, 30 minutes before bed.
- Still woke up at 3am on nights two, four and five.
- The company literally tells you it takes five to ten days, so I know I'm being impatient.
- It's 300mg magnesium glycinate. No melatonin, so there's nothing to make me feel it working.
- Which is the annoying part. There's no effect to report. Just data I don't have yet.
- I'm going to keep going to night fourteen and come back either way.
- If it doesn't work I'll say so and I'll say what I paid.
- Update at day fourteen. Save this if you want to know whether I regret it.

**On-screen text:** day 6 / woke at 3am: nights 2, 4, 5 / brand says 5-10 days / no melatonin = nothing to 'feel' / coming back at day 14

**Caption:** Day 14 update coming. Guess now: works or doesn't?  
**~26s**

**Share reason:** Sent to a friend who's mid-argument about whether supplements do anything, as a live experiment to follow  
**Comment trigger:** Asking people to predict the outcome, plus openly doubting the product, pulls in both 'give it time, mine took 10 days' and 'it's a placebo' replies arguing with each other

**Why views:** Admitting failure at day six is the opposite of what viewers expect from a product video, and the promised day-14 update turns the open loop into follows, saves and return views.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 5, retention 4, share_trigger 3, native 5, honesty 4, reusability 5

Editor fix: The open loop is deferred to a different video, so this post's own completion rate has nothing to pay off — give it an in-video micro-payoff (e.g. check the tracker on camera at the end and read out the one night that was different, or state the exact thing that would make you call it a fail) so viewers finish this one instead of only saving it.

## 6. [bench] v5 - Reading real reviews out loud and reacting to the one repeated phrase  (score 6/10)

**Hook** visual: Phone held close to camera, thumb scrolling a wall of reviews fast; scroll stops hard on a highlighted line | audio: I scrolled two thousand reviews and the same seven words keep coming up. | text: **Same 7 words. Over and over.**

**Script**
- I scrolled two thousand reviews and the same seven words keep coming up.
- 'I stopped waking up at 3am.' That one. Again. And again.
- Not 'life changing'. Not 'best sleep ever'. Just that.
- Which is weirdly the only kind of review I trust, because it's so specific.
- So I checked what's actually in it: 300mg magnesium glycinate. No melatonin.
- One capsule, 30 minutes before bed. That's the entire instruction.
- 4.7 stars across 2,100 of them, which for a supplement that does one boring thing is quite a lot.
- The reviews also warn you it takes about five to ten days, which I appreciated.
- I'm on night eleven. And I've now got the same seven words as everyone else.

**On-screen text:** "I stopped waking up at 3am" / 4.7 stars / 2,100 reviews / 300mg magnesium glycinate / no melatonin / night 11

**Caption:** Do you trust a specific review more than a glowing one?  
**~28s**

**Share reason:** Sent to the friend who always says 'reviews are fake' — the point of the video is how to read reviews, not the product  
**Comment trigger:** The claim that vague five-star reviews are less trustworthy than specific ones is a hot take people love to back up or dispute with their own examples

**Why views:** A fast review scroll that slams to a stop on one highlighted sentence is pure visual curiosity, and the repeated phrase gives the video a built-in payoff to wait for.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 4, retention 3, share_trigger 3, native 3, honesty 3, reusability 4

Editor fix: This is a proof-point recital dressed as a video — '4.7 stars across 2,100' plus ingredient plus dosage plus timeline is four ad beats in a row with nothing changing on screen. Keep the scroll-stop hook, cut the stars/dosage lines entirely, and make the whole body about why specific reviews beat glowing ones; also fix 'seven words' — the quoted phrase is six.
