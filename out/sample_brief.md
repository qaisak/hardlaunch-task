# sample_brief: ranked variants

## 1. [SHIP] v3 - 3-item ranking of reasons not to buy it, most controversial last  (score 8/10)

**Hook** visual: Creator sat on the edge of the bed, product bottle on the duvet beside them, holding up three fingers straight to camera | audio: Three reasons you shouldn't buy the sleep thing I've been taking. | text: **3 reasons not to buy it**

**Script**
- Three reasons you shouldn't buy the sleep thing I've been taking.
- One. It's slow. Five to ten days before you notice anything. If you want tonight sorted, this isn't it.
- Two. It's 24 quid a month on subscription. That's a real cost and I'm not going to pretend it isn't.
- Three, and this is the one people don't want to hear.
- It will not out-work your phone. If you're scrolling until 1am, which is exactly what you're doing right now, no capsule fixes that.
- What it is, is 300mg of magnesium glycinate with no melatonin, so you don't wake up foggy.
- The review line that made me try it was, I stopped waking up at 3am. That's the whole promise. That's it.
- So if you're still scrolling, reason three is for you.

**On-screen text:** 1. slow. 5 to 10 days / 2. £24 a month / 3. ... / it can't out-work your phone / 300mg magnesium glycinate, no melatonin / 4.7 stars, 2,100 reviews / you're still scrolling though

**Caption:** Reason three is the one nobody likes. What time is it where you are?  
**~28s**

**Share reason:** Sent to the mate who complains about sleep every week but is on TikTok at midnight, as a light callout  
**Comment trigger:** Directly accusing the viewer of scrolling at 1am provokes defensive replies, and the price named out loud starts a worth-it or not argument

**Why views:** A negative ranking about a product the creator actually uses is a contradiction the brain wants resolved, and calling out the viewer's current behaviour lands mid-scroll.

Scores: hook_strength 4, specificity 5, retention 4, share_trigger 4, native 4, honesty 5, reusability 5

Editor fix: Reason three is the whole video, so tease it in the first 1.5s instead of counting in. Lead with 'reason three is going to annoy you' over the three fingers, then run one, two, three, and cut the product spec line down to a single overlay so the middle never flattens.

## 2. [bench] v2 - Day-by-day diary where the early days are honestly boring, payoff in the last 20%  (score 7/10)

**Hook** visual: Creator in bed, lamp on, holding one capsule up to the camera, slightly annoyed face | audio: Day four of this and I've felt absolutely nothing, so I'm filming the whole thing. | text: **day 4: still nothing**

**Script**
- Day four of this and I've felt absolutely nothing, so I'm filming the whole thing.
- Day five, nothing. Slept badly, woke up at 3am as usual, brilliant.
- Day six, I checked the label to see if I'd been taking it wrong. I hadn't. One capsule, 30 minutes before bed.
- Day seven, the site does say five to ten days, so fine, I'm not out yet.
- Day eight, first night I didn't remember waking up. Could be a fluke.
- Day nine, same. Day ten, same.
- It's 300mg magnesium glycinate, there's no melatonin in it, so I'm not groggy in the morning, which was my whole worry.
- I'm not going to tell you it's magic. I'm telling you it took over a week and I nearly quit on day six.

**On-screen text:** day 5: nothing / day 6: checked I wasn't doing it wrong / day 7: label says 5 to 10 days / day 8: didn't wake up / day 9, day 10: same / no melatonin, no morning fog / day 6 is where most people quit

**Caption:** Anyone else give up on something at day six?  
**~35s**

**Share reason:** Sent by one sceptical friend to another as proof that the honest version of a supplement review exists, and as a warning not to quit early  
**Comment trigger:** The day six quit point is a plainly stated opinion people argue with, plus loads of people reply about the thing they gave up on too soon

**Why views:** Opening on failure rather than success breaks the pattern of every supplement video and buys enough scepticism credit that people stay for the turn.

Scores: hook_strength 3, specificity 4, retention 4, share_trigger 4, native 4, honesty 3, reusability 5

Editor fix: The hook is a face in bed with a capsule, visually identical to every supplement video. Open on the phone or a written day-by-day list with days 4 to 7 all reading 'nothing', so the visible stake is failure, and soften the day 8 to 10 claim to something you can honestly say rather than a clean three-night result.

## 3. [bench] v1 - Screenshot receipts, result shown before the process  (score 6/10)

**Hook** visual: Phone screen held close, sleep app history showing a wake-up at 3:04am, then 3:11am, then 2:58am, thumb scrolling through them | audio: Three fifty eight. Three eleven. Three oh four. That was my last two weeks. | text: **my 3am wake-ups, logged**

**Script**
- Three fifty eight. Three eleven. Three oh four. That was my last two weeks.
- Every night, same thing, awake for an hour, then dead by 11am at my desk.
- I didn't buy a supplement for it. I read reviews for about an hour first because I assume all of this is nonsense.
- The line that kept coming up on this one was, I stopped waking up at 3am. Same words, over and over.
- It's magnesium glycinate. 300mg. No melatonin in it, which is the bit I cared about.
- One capsule, half an hour before bed. That's the whole routine.
- Nothing happened for the first week, and I nearly binned it.
- Then I checked the app again, and that's the part I'll show you.

**On-screen text:** 1 hour of reading reviews, not an ad / 4.7 stars, 2,100 reviews / 300mg magnesium glycinate / no melatonin / day 1 to 7: nothing / then check the screen again

**Caption:** What time do you wake up in the night? Mine was 3am on the dot.  
**~30s**

**Share reason:** Someone who wakes at the same time every night sends it to their partner or group chat as, this is literally me, look at the times  
**Comment trigger:** Asking what time people wake up gets hundreds of one-word replies, and the deliberate withhold of the final screen makes people ask what happened

**Why views:** Real timestamps on a real phone read as evidence rather than advertising, and the unresolved final screen keeps people to the last beat.

Scores: hook_strength 5, specificity 4, retention 3, share_trigger 4, native 3, honesty 3, reusability 4

Editor fix: You set up the final screen and never show it. Either show the app screen in the last 3 seconds or drop the implied result entirely, and bin the 'not an ad' overlay, that line is the single biggest tell that it is an ad.
